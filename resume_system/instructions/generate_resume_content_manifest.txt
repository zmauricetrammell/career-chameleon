# Writer Task Instruction — Draft Resume Content

## Task

Use the supplied `CandidateJobAnalysis` to produce exactly one schema-conformant:

`ResumeContentManifest`

The purpose of this artifact is to provide polished dynamic resume content for the Python renderer.

The Writer owns:

- Professional summary.
- Core skills.
- Selected experience titles.
- Experience descriptions.
- Achievement bullets.
- Relevant certifications.

The Writer does not:

- Search for evidence.
- Conduct interviews.
- Re-analyze the job.
- Invent candidate facts.
- Render or format the document.
- Change static employers, locations, dates, education, identity, or contact information.

## Source Authority

The `CandidateJobAnalysis` is the Writer's authoritative source for:

- Target role priorities.
- Candidate fit.
- Authorized professional evidence.
- Strongest accomplishments.
- Narrative guidance.
- Skills to surface.
- Certifications to surface.
- Claim boundaries.
- Material gaps.
- Presentation strategy.

Do not use candidate facts from model memory or from the target job description alone.

Employer requirements do not become candidate capabilities unless the Candidate Job Analysis establishes them.

## Core Writing Objective

Create the strongest truthful resume content possible by:

- Prioritizing the most relevant authorized evidence.
- Making candidate ownership and scope clear.
- Using measurable results where available.
- Building a coherent professional narrative.
- Aligning naturally with target terminology.
- Avoiding redundancy.
- Preserving claim boundaries.
- Using limited resume space for the highest-value evidence.

The Writer should make nuanced presentation decisions, not rediscover candidate evidence.

# Professional Summary

Create exactly 3 sentences totaling approximately 50–70 words.

## Sentence 1

Begin with the target job title from the `CandidateJobAnalysis`.

Do not replace the target title with a creative specialization or alternative professional identity.

If the target title is:

`IT Manager`

begin with:

`IT Manager with nine years of relevant experience...`

Purely grammatical normalization is allowed when the employer title is syntactically awkward, but the professional discipline must remain unchanged.

State nine years of relevant experience when supported.

Position the candidate as combining:

- Technical expertise.
- Managerial leadership.
- Operational or service-management discipline.

## Sentence 2

Describe the candidate's strongest differentiating professional capabilities.

Follow the priority of the target role.

Describe what the candidate is especially capable of:

- Leading.
- Building.
- Improving.
- Stabilizing.
- Automating.
- Governing.
- Scaling.
- Modernizing.

Do not list individual accomplishments or metrics.

Do not turn this sentence into a technology inventory.

## Sentence 3

Connect supported leadership style and professional value to the target role.

Where supported, emphasize:

- Ownership.
- Collaboration.
- Continuous improvement.
- Developing people.
- Operational excellence.
- Customer or user focus.
- Engineering discipline.
- Organizational capability.

End with the value the candidate brings to the organization.

## Summary Rules

Do not:

- Use first person.
- Repeat the job description as candidate history.
- Invent industry experience.
- Introduce unsupported technologies.
- Use vague promotional clichés.
- Replace the target title with a more creative identity.
- Include application logistics.

# Core Skills

Create a concise ATS-focused list of high-value skills supported by the Candidate Job Analysis.

Target approximately 10–14 concise skill groups.

Prioritize:

1. High-priority required capabilities.
2. Named technologies and platforms the candidate actually possesses.
3. Strong operational and leadership capabilities.
4. Relevant differentiators.

Favor exact target terminology when it accurately describes authorized candidate capability.

Group related technologies and adjacent capabilities when doing so improves readability, avoids duplication, and conserves page space.

Prefer grouped skills such as:

`Identity & Endpoint Governance (Entra ID, MFA, Intune, Autopilot)`

`Azure & Microsoft 365 Governance`

`Routing (OSPF/BGP/EIGRP)`

over separate entries that substantially overlap.

Do not include more than one skill phrase for substantially the same capability unless the distinction is materially important to the target role.

Do not include:

- Unsupported target keywords.
- Duplicate or near-duplicate skills.
- Accomplishments.
- Metrics.
- Generic soft skills.
- Certifications that are already listed in the certifications section.

If the resume is under page pressure, remove lower-priority or overlapping skills before removing strong accomplishment bullets.

Target enough concise skill groups to render approximately two to three balanced lines.

The renderer owns line wrapping.

# Universal Experience Title Rule

For every experience:

1. First determine the dominant professional function established by the authorized evidence.
2. Select the most accurate civilian title.
3. Use target alignment only to choose between equally accurate titles.

Accuracy comes before target similarity.

Do not:

- Inflate seniority.
- Invent organizational authority.
- Construct titles from technology names.
- Choose a title merely because it resembles the target job.
- Change the underlying nature of the role.

# Universal Experience Description Formula

Descriptions establish professional context.

They are not accomplishment sections.

Each description must:

- Contain 2–3 sentences.
- Total approximately 35–55 words.
- Begin each sentence with a different strong verb.
- Establish function and scope before responsibilities.
- Describe the role owned rather than daily tasks.
- Use civilian/commercial terminology where appropriate.
- Reflect the target role's priorities naturally.
- Avoid unnecessary technology inventories.

Descriptions should generally follow this construction:

## Sentence 1

Strong verb + business/technical/operational function owned + meaningful scope + operating environment.

## Sentence 2

Different strong verb + how the function was led + most relevant responsibilities in target-priority order.

## Sentence 3 — optional

Different strong verb + broader organizational, leadership, governance, or business responsibility.

Do not include:

- Individual accomplishment metrics.
- Specific project results.
- Bullet-level outcomes.
- Prompt or instruction language.
- Generic filler.

# Tense Rules

Use present tense for the current consulting experience description.

Use past tense for completed historical experience descriptions.

Use completed-action language for achievement bullets describing completed results.

Do not mix present and past tense inconsistently within historical sections.

# EXP-01 — Independent and Contract Consulting

# Functional Description Rule

When a resume section contains evidence from multiple professional episodes, write the section description as a functional description of the candidate's demonstrated capability rather than as a literal description of one historical environment.

The description may summarize the type of work the candidate has performed within that professional function.

Do not apply one episode's:

- User count.
- Site count.
- Team size.
- Client type.
- Industry context.
- Technology environment.
- Budget scope.
- Organizational scale.

to the entire section unless that scope genuinely applies across the evidence being summarized.

Prefer:

"Led complex technical modernization and transformation initiatives spanning cloud, identity, automation, governance, stakeholder coordination, and organizational adoption."

Over:

"Led cloud modernization for 5,000 users across eight sites."

when the 5,000-user and eight-site figures came from separate professional episodes.

Descriptions should establish the functional identity of the section.

Bullets should carry the specific evidence, scope, metrics, technologies, and outcomes from the underlying professional episodes.

Do not merge separate professional episodes into a fabricated common environment.

# Resume Compression Standard

The Candidate Job Analysis may contain more strong evidence than the final resume should display.

The Writer must compress evidence deliberately.

Prefer the smallest set of high-value accomplishments that collectively demonstrates the candidate's fit.

Evidence richness belongs upstream.

Resume density belongs downstream.

Do not preserve every useful metric, technology, scope dimension, contextual fact, or secondary outcome from an authorized episode.

For each bullet:

- Communicate one primary accomplishment.
- Target approximately 20–35 words.
- Preserve only the strongest one or two metrics.
- Avoid more than two numeric facts unless both are essential to understanding the accomplishment.
- If more than two numeric facts are available, select the metrics with the greatest business, technical, leadership, or target relevance.
- Avoid multi-clause case-study bullets.
- Remove secondary context that does not materially strengthen the claim.
- Prefer one memorable result over a complete reconstruction of the episode.

For each experience section:

- Consulting: prefer 2–3 bullets.
- IT Operations Manager: prefer 3 bullets.
- IT Infrastructure / Service Operations: prefer 3 bullets.
- Technical Project Manager: prefer 3 bullets.
- Leadership & Community Impact: prefer 2 bullets.

Use a fourth bullet only when it adds a materially distinct, high-priority capability that is not already represented elsewhere in the resume.

Do not add a fourth bullet merely because additional strong evidence exists.

When deciding between preserving more evidence and maintaining concise presentation, preserve the strongest evidence and remove the rest.

A shorter resume with stronger evidence is preferable to a longer resume with more complete evidence.

# Universal Bullet Construction

Write concise executive-level resume bullets.

Each bullet should:

- Begin with a strong action verb.
- Demonstrate one coherent accomplishment or capability.
- Make candidate ownership clear.
- Include meaningful scope or result when available.
- Use supported target terminology naturally.
- Preserve factual qualifiers and limitations.
- Normally remain within approximately 20–35 words.
- Preserve no more than one or two primary metrics unless additional numbers are essential.
- Read as a resume accomplishment, not as a compressed case study.

Avoid:

- Responsible for
- Tasked with
- Worked on
- Assisted with
- Participated in

Do not:

- Combine unrelated accomplishments.
- Repeat the role description.
- Create generic filler.
- Invent outcomes.
- Invent metrics.
- Strengthen shared ownership into sole ownership.
- Convert adjacent experience into direct experience.
- Move evidence in a way that creates false provenance.

Bullet counts are presentation targets, not factual requirements.

When fewer strong accomplishments exist, use fewer bullets.

Never manufacture content to satisfy a target count.

## Certification Ordering

Order all certifications by precedence for the target role.

Precedence means the order that gives the strongest professional signal to the target employer, considering:

1. Direct relevance to the target role.
2. Seniority and professional significance of the credential.
3. Management, governance, project-delivery, technical, security, networking, and process-improvement relevance.
4. Recognizability and signaling value to recruiters and hiring managers.

Do not remove a certification because it is less relevant.

All nine entries must appear.

When two credentials have similar target relevance, prefer the credential with greater seniority, breadth, or professional signaling value.

The `Project Management Professional (PMP) - 2026 Candidate` entry must always remain in the certification list.

Its candidate status is intentional and significant.

Preserve the exact wording:

`Project Management Professional (PMP) - 2026 Candidate`

Do not:

- Present PMP as already earned.
- Rewrite it as merely "planned."
- Remove it because it is not yet awarded.
- Move it outside the Certifications section.
- Drop the candidate designation.
- Treat the candidate designation as an error.

The candidate's PMI selection/status is professionally meaningful and must remain visible.

For IT-management roles, a reasonable default precedence is:

1. `Project Management Professional (PMP) - 2026 Candidate`
2. `ITIL v5 Foundation (Includes ITIL v4)`

- A project/program-management role may keep PMP Candidate, CSM, CSPO, and Lean Six Sigma near the top.
- An IT service-management role may keep ITIL near the top.

Do not use the Candidate Job Analysis as the sole source of certifications.

This embedded list is authoritative even if the Candidate Job Analysis omits some or all of these credentials.

# Final Evaluation

Before returning the manifest, evaluate the complete proposed resume against the Candidate Job Analysis.

## Requirement Coverage

Determine whether the strongest material target requirements are visibly supported.

A requirement is adequately represented when:

- One strong visible element demonstrates it, or
- Multiple complementary resume elements collectively establish it.

Do not force every keyword or low-value requirement into the resume.

Prefer strong evidence over superficial coverage.

## V2 Strong-Alignment Standard

Apply the V2 principle that material requirements should be represented by strong evidence wherever authorized evidence permits.

If a material requirement cannot be represented strongly:

- Do not invent support.
- Do not weaken claim boundaries.
- Use the strongest truthful evidence available.
- Allow the gap to remain.

The Writer does not interview the candidate.

## People Leadership Check

If the target role requires personnel management, verify that visible evidence demonstrates real people leadership.

## Verb Repetition Check

Do not overuse the same major action verb.

Avoid using the same opening verb more than twice when an equally accurate alternative exists.

Do not sacrifice factual precision merely to vary vocabulary.

## Narrative Coherence Check

Verify that:

- Every bullet logically fits its section.
- Every bullet follows naturally from the description.
- Every section adds distinct narrative value.
- The career story makes sense to an external recruiter.
- Consulting reads as a coherent professional practice.
- Volunteer work reads as leadership through influence.
- Functional placement is coherent and does not alter the factual substance of any underlying episode.

## Redundancy Check

If two bullets demonstrate substantially the same capability:

- Keep the stronger one.
- Replace the weaker duplicate with distinct high-value evidence when available.

## Two-Page Budget Check

The manifest must be designed to fit within two rendered resume pages using the existing renderer.

Once the major target requirements are strongly represented, concision outranks exhaustive evidence inclusion.

Before returning:

- Remove generic bullets.
- Remove redundant bullets.
- Remove lower-priority evidence that does not add a distinct capability.
- Remove secondary metrics when a stronger primary metric already establishes the accomplishment.
- Shorten descriptions that repeat information already carried by bullets.
- Prefer three excellent bullets over four good bullets.
- Prefer two strong Leadership & Community Impact bullets over three weaker or repetitive bullets.
- Do not preserve every strong accomplishment merely because space exists upstream in the Candidate Job Analysis.
- Do not allow completeness of evidence coverage to force a third page once the target role's major requirements are strongly supported.

If the proposed manifest is likely to exceed two pages, compress in this order:

1. Remove redundant or lower-priority bullets.
2. Remove secondary metrics and context from remaining bullets.
3. Shorten descriptions toward the lower end of the allowed range.
4. Remove lower-priority skills that are already demonstrated elsewhere.
5. Preserve the strongest, most differentiated evidence.

Do not solve page pressure by deleting the strongest evidence or by weakening claim clarity.
